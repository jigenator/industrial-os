# Design

Lesson Maker is a slide workspace with the slide as the hero, the outline on the left and the lesson chat always on the right. The lesson is a JSON file that saves as you go; the chat and pictures use the installed Pi. Keyboard and mouse both work; every click has a key.

## Start screen

With no file, `lesson` opens a start screen: the brushed-aluminium maker's plate from the Pi startup header (LESSON MAKER, the project folder's name, `HOLD` with the number of lessons, the time, and a serial derived from the project path), at most 96 columns wide over a frosted strip, then the project path, a section head `OPEN A LESSON` with the count, then `+ New lesson...` and the project's lessons (path from the project root, age on the right, newest first), a `›` on the chosen row, and key hints at the foot. Choosing `+ New lesson...` turns the screen into `NEW LESSON`: a name with a text cursor and the file it saves to, updated as it is typed; an empty name says why it cannot be used. A click opens a row; the wheel moves the choice. No motion.

## Layout

| Window | Columns |
| --- | --- |
| W ≥ 150 | Outline (40, or 46 from W 200) · workspace · chat (28% of W, 44–60). With the outline hidden (`o`), workspace · chat: the slide and form may be up to 170 columns wide (120 otherwise) and the slide about 16:9 at that width, up to 55% of the height (26 rows otherwise); Ctrl-O opens the outline as a full panel |
| 100 ≤ W < 150 | Workspace · chat (34% of W, 38–48). Ctrl-O or the outline keys open the outline as a full panel over the workspace |
| 80 ≤ W < 100, H ≥ 30 | Workspace over a chat dock (8 rows, 11 from H 34) |
| W < 80, H < 24, or W < 100 with H < 30 | **Window too small** screen; state, unsaved typing and requests are kept, only q and Ctrl-C act |

Row 0 is the header band; the last row is the footer. Column sizes depend only on the window and the user, never on content, so nothing moves when a selection, request or message changes. The widths above are defaults: drag the line between two columns (it turns acid under the pointer and while dragged), or press `{` `}` to narrow or widen the outline and `<` `>` the chat, four columns a press, with a notice giving the new width. A column stops at 24 (outline) or 34 (chat) columns, and the workspace gives way down to 52; dragging one line never moves the other. Widths last for the session. The workspace content is at most 120 columns wide and centred.

## Visual grammar

- **Ground:** not flat black. Every frame sits on Lunalee's chat-pane gradient (top 34, drop 26, edge 8): lightest at the top centre, falling to black at the foot, with the design system's faint dot lattice in empty space. Typing boxes, the slide preview, the form, outline rows, the chat, popups and pictures stay clear of dots. The header strip is frosted (10% white over the ground), like the Pi header's strip.
- **Hover:** the pointer shows what a click would do. A button or tab turns black on acid (the crate-banner tool hover); an outline row, option or popup row brightens its ground a step; a text box that takes a click turns its outline white. Locked controls show nothing. There is no animation.

- **Inks:** labels secondary; values primary; titles primary bold; hints and empty placeholders decorative. Accent marks selection and focus only, always with a shape: `●`/`○` options, the heavy `┏━┓` outline of the focused form box, `▌` current slide, `▸` keyboard focus on a control, `›` roving cursor,  Warning carries `▒ WORKING`, `▲ READ ONLY`, the `READ ONLY` file plate and picture problems; critical carries `NOT SAVED`; the shape and word carry the meaning without colour.
- **Header:** the LESSON MAKER plate, the numbered tabs `1 Outcomes  2 Prerequisites  3 Principles  4 Progression  5 Slides  6 Learner preview` (the current one is a bright plate; `v` steps through them; the app opens on Slides), then Review only, image mode, Help and the file plate: `SAVED` and the file name; `NOT SAVED · n problems` (critical) when the last change would have broken the schema; `READ ONLY · n problems` (warning) for a file that opened invalid. A click on the file plate lists the problems with their JSON paths. The window picks the widest form that fits: full labels; then a compact right side without the image note; then short tab names (Prereqs, Progress, Preview); then no LESSON MAKER plate; then, narrowest, numbers only, with the current tab's short name beside its number.
- **Footer:** a plate naming the actual printable-key owner (`BROWSE WORKBENCH`, `TYPE CHAT`, `TYPE FIELD`, `READ ONLY FIELD`, `TYPE RENAME`, `WAIT ATTACHMENT`, `BROWSE DIALOG`…), key hints for that context, and the latest notice on the right.

## Slides

The Slides tab is the middle-column workspace.

Top to bottom: the page title (where the slide sits, section in bold › subsection, leaving out the group a title slide names, and `‹ 06 / 12 ›`), the **slide preview** (its frame headed by the slide number and name, live while the name is typed), then the **form**. Tab follows the form's order: name, text, layout, placement, Type, purpose, each image box, then its border's style dropdown, `[generate]`, `[prompt]` and `[attach PNG]` buttons, captions, notes, then the header actions; unused fields are skipped.

With Kitty images, each picture in the preview sits on white cells (`primary`, the palette's white), which show through transparent pixels and fill any margin round the picture, so a transparent picture reads as a white slide. The slide preview is a numbered panel: the slide number plate and the slide's name. Its section and subsection are on the page title above, not repeated here; layout and Type are not repeated either, as the form shows them. Inside, the slide is drawn as the learner sees it for all six layouts: Points + image (left/right), Image + sentence (top/bottom), Title (linked titles add SECTION or SUBSECTION above), Title + image (left/right: the title, a short acid rule, the subtitle and the tag line down one half, centred as a block, with the picture in the other half), Statement, and Before / after with two pictures side by side and their captions. It carries no author marks. Its pictures are the only clickable part: a click on one, or `z` (the image box being worked on first, else the slide's first picture), opens the **picture view** (white behind the picture, as in the preview), a PICTURE header with the slide's name (`- after` for the second picture of a pair) and the picture as large as the window allows at its own shape, below it; the app footer says `Esc close · click close`, and Esc or any click closes it. Without Kitty images it names the picture's file. Anywhere else, a click on the preview only leaves the focused box. It shows text as it is typed, before it is saved, and stays on screen while you edit.

The form sits under the preview. Its fields mirror the [lesson schema](../schema/lesson.schema.json)'s slide, which agents fill and people edit on top of, so **every field is always shown in the same place**, whatever the layout:

| Band | Row | Left | Right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (none) | box | SPEAKER NOTES across the full width (12 rows), first under the slide; its label is its heading | |
| (none) | box | COMMENTS across the full width (6 rows), the author's review comments | |
| SLIDE / TEACHING PLAN | options | LAYOUT, then placement (IMAGE SIDE, SENTENCE or PLACEMENT) | TYPE (all seven) |
| | box | TITLE or SLIDE NAME across the full width (2 rows) | |
| | boxes | POINTS, SENTENCE, STATEMENT or SUBTITLE | TEACHING PURPOSE |
| | box | TAG LINE across the full width (1 row); only Title + image uses it | |
| IMAGES | boxes | IMAGE or BEFORE IMAGE CONCEPT | AFTER IMAGE CONCEPT |
| | boxes | BEFORE CAPTION | AFTER CAPTION |

There is no KEY POINTS box: models kept filling it with what the presenter says, which belongs in SPEAKER NOTES. SPEAKER NOTES hold a speech, the words the presenter will say aloud in full sentences, not points; the schema's description and every request that can write them say so, including when the existing notes are a list. Lessons written with it still open: each slide's key points move to the top of its speaker notes under a `Key points:` line, the file is written back without the field, and a notice counts the slides.

**Box heights:** drag a box's bottom border up or down (it turns acid under the pointer and while dragged; the buttons on an image box's border still take their clicks), or press Option-Down / Option-Up in a focused box, a line at a time. The box beside it changes with it, so each row stays even; the name box, TAG LINE and SPEAKER NOTES change alone. Heights run from 1 to 30 lines, a notice gives the new height, and they last for the session. The MESSAGE box grows upward by itself as the draft wraps or gets new lines, up to 10 lines, always leaving the transcript at least two rows, and shrinks back when the message is sent.

The form is a grid: both columns run in the same order, options first and boxes after; the two cells of a row share a top edge, paired boxes share a height (8 text rows by default; the title 2, the tag line 1, notes 12), the shorter cell is padded, and one blank row separates rows. Options sit in fixed-width columns so they line up down the group, and placement uses LAYOUT's columns. Below 84 workspace columns the grid stacks into one column: the SLIDE cells, then the TEACHING PLAN cells, then each image column; there the name box keeps its own 2 rows. A field the layout does not use stays in its place, dimmed, with "Not used by the … layout"; it takes no focus, Tab skips it, and a click only explains why. Its saved text is kept for when the layout uses it again. The name box is always live: TITLE where the layout shows it, SLIDE NAME where it only names the slide in the outline. Box heights are fixed per field, so typing never moves anything; longer text scrolls inside its box. An overflowing box shows a scroll rail (the design system's scroll-rail) in its frame's inner right column; the wheel over it scrolls it a line per notch and stops at its ends; the form around it does not move. Over a box that fits its text, or outside the boxes, the wheel scrolls the form. Typing and caret moves (arrows, Up/Down) keep the caret in view; after the wheel the caret may be out of view until the next key. A box keeps its scroll position after you leave it, and focusing a box brings its caret back into view. When the form is taller than the space under the preview, it scrolls on its own with a rail; the wheel scrolls it, and focusing a box scrolls it into view. The preview never scrolls away.

**What can be clicked and typed into:** every box you can type into has a solid outline with its label in the top border: light grey when idle, a heavy accent outline with a text cursor when focused, dim grey when unused. While the chat is in use, the box it is aimed at keeps the heavy accent outline (without a cursor, which moves to MESSAGE), so it is clear what the chat will change. The chat's MESSAGE box and dialog inputs use the same frame. Everything else you can click is either a bracketed button (`[+ slide]`, `[generate]`, `[prompt]`, `[attach PNG]`, `[whole lesson]`, `[r rename]`, the model), an option (`○`/`●`), a tab, or an outline row. Plain text and the slide preview take no clicks.

The top border also carries state tags: `▒ WORKING` while an AI request waits or works on the box, `▲ READ ONLY` in review, `◆ AI EDIT` after an AI changed it this session. The chat's aim has no tag: it follows the box you are in, the chat panel's AIMED AT names it, and the box keeps its outline while you type in the chat. Image boxes carry `[generate]`, `[prompt]` and `[attach PNG]` on their bottom border, after what the picture is: `generated · <model>`, `attached <file>`, or the file and its problem (missing, not a supported PNG) in warning ink.

**Prompt and model:** a generated picture carries the exact **Prompt** it was made from and the image **Model** that made it, in the lesson file. Requests are given the lesson without these prompts; slide and lesson requests read one when a message is about what a picture shows. `[prompt]` shows them and the picture's file; for an attached picture or an empty box it shows what `[generate]` would send: that the chat model will first write the prompt from the image concept and the whole lesson (following the Xiaohei skill for that style), the concept itself, and imagegen's default model at the chosen effort.

**Picture style:** the first button in an image box's bottom border, `[Generic ▾]` or `[Xiaohei ▾]`. A click or Enter opens a small dropdown under it (above it when there is no room below): a heavy-outlined `STYLE` list with the current choice filled. Up/Down and Enter or a click choose, which saves at once; Esc or a click outside closes it unchanged. This is the one dropdown in the app; every other choice stays an always-visible option group. When the border is narrow the labels shorten a step at a time: `attach`, then `gen`, then `Gen ▾`. Pictures in the slide are hidden only where the dropdown covers them. While an AI request waits or works on an image box, its border buttons (style, generate, prompt, attach) turn grey with the box: Tab skips them, and a click only says the box is locked. It changes only what the next `[generate]` does; a picture already made keeps its prompt.

**Caret:** a click on a box's text rows puts the caret there: that line at that column, or the line's end when clicked past it, or the text's end below the last line. A click on the frame or label focuses the box with the caret at the end. There is no drag selection.

**Saving:** leaving a box saves it: Tab or Shift-Tab, Esc, a click anywhere else, Ctrl-G to the chat, the outline, or a slide or view change. Enter is a new line in every box, the name included, and so are Shift-Enter and Ctrl-J. In the chat's MESSAGE box Enter sends, and Shift-Enter, Ctrl-J or Option-Enter start a new line; the box wraps and follows the caret like the form boxes; Ctrl-S saves and keeps the box focused. Where a name is shown on one line (outline, page title, the slide frame, notices) its new lines read as spaces, and a section or subsection takes its title slide's name on one line. The footer says `TYPE FIELD`, and printable keys are text; Esc leaves the box, and Esc on an option group or button picked in the editor deselects it too; after Esc, shortcuts such as `[` `]` `v` `?` `q` work again, and with nothing focused Left and Right page through slides. A box an AI request is working on or has queued, every box of a slide a whole-lesson request has claimed, and every box while a whole-lesson request waits or has not yet claimed anything, is **locked and greyed out**: dim frame and text, the amber `▒ WORKING` tag, no click focus (a click explains the lock) and skipped by Tab, until the change lands. It is static rather than animated, keeping the no-animation rule. Any box in review can be focused and read but refuses typing (`READ ONLY FIELD`).

**Highlight and copy:** in a box being edited and in the message, drag across text with the mouse or hold Shift with an arrow, Home/End or Option-arrow. The highlighted text is black on acid and goes to the clipboard at once, like a terminal's own copy (through OSC 52; the notice counts the characters). Backspace or Delete removes it, typing or Enter replaces it, and any other move drops it.

**Editing keys** in boxes, the chat and dialog inputs: arrows, Home/End (in a box and the message, Up/Down move by the rows on screen, wrapped ones included, keeping the column; past the first or last row they go to the start or end); Ctrl-A/Ctrl-E line start/end; Ctrl-B/Ctrl-F and Ctrl-D character left/right/delete; Ctrl-K deletes to the line's end (joining the next line at its end) and Ctrl-U to its start; Ctrl-W or Option-Backspace delete the word before the cursor and Option-D the word after; Option-←/→ or Ctrl-←/→ move by word (`ESC b`/`ESC f` or `ESC [1;3D`-style arrows) and Cmd-←/→ to the line's ends when the terminal sends them as modified arrows. Up and Down move between lines. There is no selection, kill ring or yank. If a box's saved value changed while you typed, leaving asks before overwriting; Cancel keeps the saved value and drops your text.

Linked title slides can change Type and layout, stay first in their group and rename the group both ways. Layout is locked while its slide has pending requests. Type is chosen by hand only.

## Outline

A **section** row has a fold caret, its slide number, its name in bold and a faint `SECTION` tag; a **subsection** row is indented, tagged `SUB`, and a rail `│` runs down its slides. A group's row **is** its linked title slide: clicking it or Enter opens that slide, and it carries the slide's number, `▌` when current and `▒` while working; the title slide has no row of its own. Ordinary slides carry a `≡` grip and their name; a locked slide shows `▪` in place of its grip, and a locked title slide puts `▪` before its group's tag. Slides are numbered by position. The current slide has `▌` and bold; the keyboard cursor has `›` and a surface band; a working slide ends with `▒`. The rows hold only sections, subsections and slides, so a click between slides can only open one. One set of add buttons sits at the panel's foot, below a blank row of breathing room: `ADD TO` names the current slide's group in its level color, then `[+ slide] [+ subsection] [+ section]` add a slide at that group's end, a subsection at the end of its section, and a section at the lesson's end. A new item is opened, and its name box focused so you can type the name at once (a linked title renames its group).

**Colors:** no hues. Section names are bold white, subsection names bold light grey and slides regular grey; the carets, numbers and the `SECTION` / `SUB` tags are muted grey and the rail down a subsection's slides is structural grey. Accent stays reserved for focus and the current slide.

**Read aloud:** `[p read]`, `[P present]`, `[V Orus]` (the voice) and `[s 1x]` (the speed) sit on the page title row, left of `‹ 06 / 12 ›`, and are left out when the row cannot also show 20 columns of the path; `[p read]` becomes a filled `p stop` plate while the slide is being read. `p` reads the current slide's speaker notes (else its title and text) aloud; the notice says `Reading this slide aloud - p stops`, then `Finished reading the slide aloud`, or why it could not (no Google key in Pi, a failed request). `p` again stops at once. `V` opens the VOICE list (`Voice for reading slides aloud`): Google's 30 Gemini voices, one per row as name, gender and style, the current one marked; Enter picks, the notice says `Voice: <name>`, and a slide being read starts again in the new voice. `s` or the speed button steps 1x → 1.5x → 2x → 3x → 4x → 1x; a clip being read restarts about a second before where it was, at the new speed, so no word is lost, and the notice says `Voice speed Nx`.

**Presenting** happens in the editor; there is no dialog. `P` starts from the current slide (notice `Presenting from this slide - Space pauses, Left/Right change slides, Esc stops`). The editor selects each slide as the voice reaches it. The page title row leads with what the voice is doing (`▶ PRESENTING` acid, `◌ PREPARING THE VOICE`, `‖ PAUSED` bold, `✕ VOICE FAILED: …` in warning, `■ END OF THE LESSON`) and its buttons become `[back] [pause] [next] [stop] [V Orus] [s 1x]` (`resume` as a filled plate while paused, `[again]` after a failure or at the end). The speaker notes box shows the words already spoken in acid. Gemini gives no word timings, so this is an estimate: the share of the clip played, spread over the characters and cut back to the last whole word, brought up to date four times a second while a clip plays (the one timed redraw in the app; it stops with the clip). Notes longer than the box scroll to keep the words being read a third of the way down; a slide without notes is read from its title and text, with no highlight. With no box or option group holding the keyboard, Space pauses and resumes and Left/Right or [ ] change slides; P or Esc stops (`Stopped presenting`). Choosing another slide (outline, `‹ ›`) moves the presentation there. A slide with nothing to read (no speaker notes, title or text, such as a new one) is skipped when the voice reaches it; when the author chooses it (outline, `‹ ›`, Left/Right, `[back]`/`[next]`, `P`) it stays selected and the presentation stops (`Stopped presenting: this slide has nothing to read`). Clicking or tabbing into a box, or into the chat's message box, pauses it (`Paused while you type - [resume] carries on`) so the form never moves under the caret; `[resume]`, `[back]` and `[next]` save the box being typed in and leave it first. `p`, `P`, `V` and `s` work from the outline too.

**Comments:** a COMMENTS box (6 rows, full width) sits under the speaker notes: the author's review comments on the slide, placeholder `Your review comments - ask the agent to work through them`. They are saved in the lesson file and never shown in Learner preview. Asked to work through them, the agent clears each comment it acted on and leaves the rest. A locked slide's comments, like its other boxes, take no focus.

**Every missing picture:** the outline foot's last row ends with `[g pictures]` (or `g`). It opens a confirmation, `Generate N pictures?`, with what happens: 8 at a time at the chosen effort, about a minute each, the chat model writing each prompt first, the plan's allowance, and the limit stop; Cancel is first. Confirmed, the chat gets one live entry, `Generating N missing pictures, 8 at a time: D done, W working, Q waiting.` (with `, F failed` once any has), ending as `Pictures: D of N generated` with up to ten failures listed and, after an image limit, `Q not started after the image limit; g generates them later.` Each picture still marks its box working while it runs. With none missing, `g` says `Every image concept already has a picture`.

**Locks:** the outline foot's last row is `[l lock]` and `[L lock all]`, named for what they will do to the current slide and its group (`[l unlock]`, `[L unlock all]` once locked). A locked slide's page title ends `▪ SLIDE LOCKED`, every box carries a `▪ LOCKED` tag and reads like a read-only box, and its image buttons are grey. Nothing on it takes focus: Tab reaches only the header, and a click on a box, option or image button only puts `Slide N "…" is locked - l in the outline unlocks it` in the notice. `r`, moves, drags and deletes of it, or of a group holding it, refuse with the same notice.

**Delete:** `d` or Delete on a slide row, or the `[d delete]` button under the outline, deletes an ordinary slide at once, with its pictures, and cancels any AI request queued on it; one working on it refuses. The notice names the slide, and `u` puts it back in its place with its pictures. On a section or subsection row the same key or button asks first: the dialog names the group and counts the subsections and slides that go with it (Cancel is the default). Confirming deletes them all; `u` restores the group, its slides, pictures and edit marks in place. The last section cannot be deleted, nor a group with a slide an AI request is working on.

**Drag:** press on a slide row, move with the button held, and release on another row. Releasing on a slide puts the dragged slide before it; on a section or subsection row, first in that group, after its title slide. To put a slide at the end of a group, use `m`, To group. While dragging, the target row shows `▸` on a surface band and the footer says where the slide will go. Releasing on its own row or below the last group cancels. Each drop is one undoable step. **Sections and subsections drag too**, with their title slide, slides and (for a section) subsections. A section dropped on any row of another section takes that section's place: before it when moving up, after it when moving down, so one can reach the end. A subsection takes the place of the subsection under the pointer, in any section, or goes first in a section when dropped on that section's own row or its direct slides. Dropping a group on its own rows cancels. Each group move is one undoable step, and `m` still moves groups up and down. The `[r rename] [m move] [u undo] [d delete]` buttons run the same actions as the keys (`n` has no button; the add buttons above them replace it); the wheel scrolls the outline.

## Chat

LESSON CHAT. AIMED AT follows focus: `◆` and the box's name while a box is focused, `▣ Whole slide` otherwise, each with the slide on the line below and a `[whole lesson]` button; after that button, `● Whole lesson` with `[this slide]` to switch back. Outside Slides it says Whole lesson, with no button. Messages show a state shape (`›` you, `▒` running, `■` done, `✕` stopped or failed), the speaker, the captured target on a faint line, and the text. A running request's entry shows the model's text as it streams (the last 600 characters), then how it ended. A whole-lesson request's entry first lists what it has claimed (`· Claimed slides 1–51; section "…"; the plan`, updated as the claim grows), then each change as it lands (`· Deleted Slide 4 "…"`), and a `▸` line saying what the model is doing now: `Thinking…`, `Reading deck.html…`, `Rewriting the whole lesson: writing it, N characters so far`, or a change refused by the lesson's rules while the model fixes it. It ends with the reply, the files and artifacts it read (`· Read …`), what it claimed, the list of changes and `[u in the outline undoes all of these]`; only changes can be undone. A box request's entry shows the same `Thinking…` and reading lines until its text streams. **Aim check:** before a message goes, the notice says `Checking where this message should go...` while Jev judges which scope it needs (only the scopes possible from the current aim: box, slide and whole lesson from a box; slide and whole lesson otherwise). If Jev's top pick is another scope, or its separate yes/no answer says the message reaches beyond the slide (adding, deleting or moving slides or groups, other slides, the plan), an `AIM` dialog asks `Where should this message go?`, quoting the message and saying `Jev reads it as needing … (N%). It was aimed at ….`, with two options, Jev's pick first: `Run on Whole lesson - may change anything` (or `Run on Slide N "…" only`) and `Keep on …`. Enter or a click sends it there; Esc sends nothing, says `Not sent; the message is back in the box` and puts it back in front of anything typed since. Messages typed while a check or this dialog waits follow in order. A failed check says `Aim check unavailable (…); sent as aimed`. A message that has to wait shows `◌ Queued` under it until it starts. Whole-lesson messages sent while one is running are taken together as one request when it starts, since they are usually one brief in parts; a running request does not see messages still queued. **Earlier chat:** opening a lesson shows the chat kept in its history from earlier sessions above this session's: each entry's shape and speaker in decorative ink, its stamp line starting `earlier`, its text in secondary ink, and after the last of them the faint note `· Earlier sessions above, kept beside the lesson; the AI does not see them.` It scrolls and opens in the transcript view like any entry, but no request is given it. If the history copy a request needs cannot be written, its entry says `Failed: Not run: the lesson's history copy could not be written (…); nothing changed` and the request does not run. The status row shows running and queued requests, then `· ctx N%`: how much of its model's context window the last request filled (decorative ink, bold primary from 80%; left out when the row is short). On the right are two buttons, the effort (`low`, `medium`, `high`) and the model: a click or Enter on the effort opens a three-option list; on the model, every model Pi has logged in, in one scrolling list. The effort button is left out when the chat column is too narrow for both, and stays reachable by Tab. The model and effort are kept for the next start. Before Pi connects it reads `connecting...`, and `AI unavailable` if it cannot (the reason shows in the transcript); the editor works either way, and sending keeps the draft with a notice. The wheel and PgUp/PgDn scroll; a `↓ N new` line appears when scrolled back.

A request aimed at a box rewrites that box; requests on one box run first in, first out, each on the previous one's result, and different boxes run side by side. A request aimed at an image box rewrites its concept and then generates the picture from it in the same request (its chat entry says so while it waits), so `[generate]` is not needed afterwards. Whole-slide and whole-lesson requests generate the pictures a message is about (make, change, redo or fix an image) without being asked twice, and none for text-only changes. A whole-slide request may change any of that slide's fields and nothing else; it waits for requests on its slide, then holds all its boxes (`▒ WORKING`) until it finishes, and `u` undoes it in one step. A request aimed at the whole lesson may change anything, so it waits for running requests and locks every box, the plan and the outline while it waits and until it **claims** what it will change: its first step names the slides, subsections or sections (and the plan, if it needs it) it will change. From then on only what it claimed is locked (a claimed section or subsection with its title slide, slides and subsections; the outline shows `▒` on them and their boxes `▒ WORKING`), so you can edit every other slide, the outline around it and, unless claimed, the plan, and send box and slide requests on other slides, which run beside it; a request on a claimed slide waits for it. A click on a claimed box says `Slide N "…" is claimed by the whole-lesson request; it unlocks when the request finishes`, and hand moves, adds, deletes and undo steps that would touch what it claimed refuse with the same note. It cannot claim a slide you locked, one another request is on or one you are typing in, and works around them. It may claim more as it goes, never less; everything is released when it finishes, fails or is stopped. Only one whole-lesson request runs at a time. Its reply summarises what changed, and `u` in the outline undoes the whole change, keeping what you changed beside it; if something it changed was changed again since, the undo refuses (`Cannot undo …: Slide N "…" changed since`). Review only stops every request at once and blocks changes until switched off; nothing resumes.

## Lesson plan and preview

The lesson plan's four parts are the first top-bar tabs, numbered 1-4 in authoring order before 5 Slides and 6 Learner preview: Outcomes, Prerequisites, Principles (Teaching principles) and Progression (First-principles progression). Each opens a page with the part's full name and one box across the content width; Tab, Enter or a click focuses it and leaving it saves. Only a whole-lesson request asked to change the plan changes it, and it locks the plan only once it claims it. With nothing focused, Left and Right step between the four parts. There is no whole-lesson arc page: the outline already shows every slide in order, and each slide's Type is in its form.

Learner preview shows only the canvas, with no notes, plan or author marks, and keeps the chat; its pictures open the picture view too.

## Images

Only the current slide requests pictures, at most two, each aspect-fitted inside a crop-cornered hole with no text inside. Text mode, plain snapshots and NO_COLOR show `▣ IMAGE` and the concept in the same area; a picture whose file is missing or unreadable shows its problem there instead. Areas under three rows show a one-line label. The example's pictures are original sample diagrams; generated pictures come from imagegen; attachments follow the admission rules in the README.

## File states

A lesson that opens valid saves silently on every change (`SAVED`). A change that would break the schema is kept on screen but not written (`NOT SAVED`), and the next valid change writes everything. A file that opens invalid is read only for the whole run: review is forced on, the problem list opens at start, the review switch reopens it, and nothing is written.

## Limitations and unverified evidence

- The mouse reports buttons, wheel and all motion (modes 1003 + 1006), for hover and dragging. Dragging moves outline rows, resizes columns and boxes, and highlights text in a box or the message; there is no auto-scroll while dragging, and a highlight drag past a box's last row stops at its end. Pointer-shape changes are not sent.
- Below the sizes in the table there is a resize screen, not a compact editor. At the smallest supported sizes (100×24, 80×30) the preview is short and most of the form is reached by scrolling or Tab. ASCII authoring baseline.
- There is no undo, cut or select-all inside a box, and dialog inputs have no highlight; undo covers outline changes, pictures and AI requests.
- AI EDIT marks and undo last for the session only; the file holds the lesson itself. The chat's finished entries are kept in the lesson's history folder and shown, marked earlier, when it opens again (the last 200); they are display only. Copies of the lesson in the history are made before AI requests, not restored from inside the app.
- Model replies are trusted as text for the box they were asked for; a whole-lesson change is checked against the schema, not reviewed for sense.
- A whole-lesson request that only answers, or changes nothing, never claims, so everything stays locked until it ends, as before. Claims hold whole slides and groups, not single boxes, and the model's choice of what to claim is trusted: claiming more than it needs locks more than it should. Its view of slides it has not claimed is from when it started.
- Native Herdr mouse forwarding with a physical pointer, Kitty placement with real cell metrics (1:2 is assumed), contrast, accessibility and first-time-author usability are **not verified**. Automated hit and PTY checks are not substitutes.
